The Agile Ant:
An iconic design in the wild

Daniel Shamsoddini1David Matthews1Chen Yu1Sam Kriegman1

1Center for Robotics and Biosystems, Northwestern University

Paper Code Gallery Press Contact

Abstract

One of the most popular testbeds for learning locomotion policies is a simulated agent called “ant”, which incidentally does not in any way, shape, or form resemble the insect. It is roughly the size of a giant panda and consists of four spherocylinder legs distributed with radial symmetry about a spherical torso. This raises several questions. Here we consider the relevance of this strange but iconic simulated design for physical robots. We trace its history in the literature, realize a scale replica (55%), and explore the challenges and opportunities of its unique body plan. We find that with certain components and attendant system identification the physical instantiation is capable of agile locomotion in the wild, somersaulting, freely spinning on its sphere, running upside down and right side up—and doing so forward, backward, and sideways, equally well, at top speed. We provide detailed instructions for building the Agile Ant in addition to the grounded simulation environment, which we hope can serve as a bridge between the classic machine learning benchmark and robotics.
